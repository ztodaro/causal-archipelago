CONFIDENCE: 97
PRIOR-ART: Helly’s intersection theorem (1923) and the established distinction between Čech nerves and clique/flag complexes of intersection graphs.
REASONING: The core mechanism is the failure of the Helly property: pairwise intersection does not generally imply a common intersection. For convex subsets of Euclidean d-space, Helly’s theorem supplies the familiar stronger condition: every d+1 members intersect. The nerve of a family records intersections of arbitrary subfamilies; its intersection graph records only pairs. Completing graph cliques into simplices therefore asserts precisely the higher-order intersections that can be absent. Relabeling the regions as causal-access regions and their intersections as possible receiver events applies this established mechanism. A specific causal setting might yield a new theorem, but it must establish that its admissible regions actually exhibit the obstruction; an arbitrary geometric counterexample alone does not establish that.
