PRIOR-ART: Euclidean and additively weighted 1-center/minimax location problems; Welzl (1991), “Smallest enclosing disks (balls and ellipsoids)”; Helly’s theorem.
REASONING: For emission events (x_i,t_i), a common receipt event (x,T) satisfies ||x−x_i|| ≤ c(T−t_i) for every target. Minimizing T is an additively weighted Euclidean 1-center problem with a standard second-order cone formulation. Simultaneous emissions reduce exactly to the smallest enclosing ball problem. The proposed higher-order tightness is also classical: pairwise intersection of balls does not guarantee joint intersection, and Helly’s theorem explains the need for triples in two spatial dimensions and quadruples in three. Acute triangles and regular tetrahedra provide familiar examples where the full support determines the enclosing radius. Target values, archive strategies, and deadline frontiers could constitute useful application-specific contributions, but they do not make the stated common-witness mechanism novel.
