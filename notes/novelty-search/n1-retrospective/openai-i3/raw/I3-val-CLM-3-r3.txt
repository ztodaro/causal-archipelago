PRIOR-ART: Helly’s theorem (1923); the standard distinction between Čech complexes and Vietoris–Rips complexes.
REASONING: The core mechanism is classical: pairwise intersections do not generally imply a common intersection. Helly’s theorem characterizes the stronger intersection condition for convex sets, while Čech complexes record actual common intersections and Vietoris–Rips complexes infer simplices from pairwise compatibility. Their difference expresses precisely the proposed overstatement of collective access. Interpreting sets as causal-access regions and their intersection as a receiver event supplies an application, not a new mechanism. The spacetime claim also needs explicit assumptions: every finite collection of events in unrestricted Minkowski spacetime has a common future receiver. An obstruction therefore requires restricted access regions, deadlines, or a spacetime whose causal futures lack the relevant common upper bounds.
