PRIOR-ART: Helly’s theorem (1923); the distinction between Čech complexes (common intersections) and Vietoris–Rips complexes (pairwise compatibility), applied to sensor networks by de Silva and Ghrist in “Coverage in sensor networks via persistent homology” (2007).
REASONING: The core mechanism is the classical failure of pairwise intersection to imply a joint intersection. In a nerve or Čech complex, all pairwise edges need not entail a simplex representing a common witness; a clique construction supplies that simplex without establishing the witness. This directly anticipates the proposed correction from pairwise compatibility to collective access. Applying it to records and spacetime provides an interpretation, not a new mechanism. The physical application also needs explicit restrictions: any finite collection of events in unrestricted Minkowski spacetime has a common future event. A relevant counterexample therefore must specify causal geometry, receiver constraints, or bounded access regions that permit the obstruction.
