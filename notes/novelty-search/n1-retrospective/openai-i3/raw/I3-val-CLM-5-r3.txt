PRIOR-ART: The smallest-enclosing-ball / Euclidean 1-center problem, including Elzinga and Hearn’s “Geometrical Solutions for Some Minimax Location Problems” (1972), together with Helly’s theorem for convex intersections.
REASONING: For target records available at events (x_i,t_i), a common receipt event (x,t) must satisfy ||x−x_i|| ≤ c(t−t_i) for every included record. Minimizing t is a standard convex minimax location problem, expressible as a second-order cone program. Simultaneous availability reduces exactly to the smallest-enclosing-ball problem. The claimed higher-order tightness is already its support geometry: a triangle or tetrahedron can determine the enclosing ball even when pairwise bounds are weaker. Helly’s theorem explains why pairwise intersection does not establish joint feasibility. Target values add subset selection and deadline-dependent reward frontiers, while collector strategies add logistics constraints. These may provide useful application-specific results, but the stated common-witness mechanism and higher-order structure are anticipated.
