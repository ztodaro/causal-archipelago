PRIOR-ART: Helly’s theorem (Eduard Helly, 1923); the Čech-complex versus Vietoris–Rips-complex distinction, applied to sensor networks by Vin de Silva and Robert Ghrist, “Coverage in sensor networks via persistent homology” (2007).
REASONING: The core mechanism is the classical failure of the pairwise intersection property to guarantee a total intersection. A Čech complex records common intersections of entire collections; a clique or Vietoris–Rips construction fills simplices from pairwise relations. Their difference already formalizes precisely the proposed overstatement of collective compatibility. Helly’s theorem identifies additional geometric conditions and intersection orders sufficient for a common witness. Reinterpreting the regions as causal access regions supplies a domain application, not a new mechanism. A spacetime example also needs explicit restrictions: finitely many events in unrestricted Minkowski spacetime always have a common future receiver, so the proposed obstruction cannot arise there without additional constraints.
