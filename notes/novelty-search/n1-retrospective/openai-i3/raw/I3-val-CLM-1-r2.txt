PRIOR-ART: Andrews, Chanamé & Agüeros, “Wide binaries as a critical test for chemical tagging” (2018), using Gaia-selected pairs and RAVE/LAMOST spectroscopy; Kamdar et al., “Stars that Move Together Were Born Together” (2019).
REASONING: The proposed mechanism is already established: common birth material predicts chemical similarity, which can independently test associations selected through stellar kinematics. Andrews and collaborators compared metallicities within wide pairs using spectroscopic survey cross-matches and chance-pair comparisons. Kamdar and collaborators extended the common-origin argument to nearby comoving stars, including associations beyond ordinary bound binaries. These precedents anticipate both the chemical certification principle and its implementation through abundance differences relative to unrelated pairs. More careful control matching, larger catalogs, or a particular separation interval could improve the measurement, but they do not introduce a new core mechanism.
