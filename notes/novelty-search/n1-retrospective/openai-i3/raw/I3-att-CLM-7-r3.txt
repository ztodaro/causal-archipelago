PRIOR-ART: Chris Argyris, Robert Putnam, and Diana McLain Smith, Action Science (1985); Chris Argyris, Knowledge for Action (1993).
REASONING: Action science supplies a substantive theory of how defensive reasoning obstructs learning and how alternative governing commitments enable testable inquiry. It operationalizes that theory through recorded interactions, explicit inference reconstruction, and public testing. Crucially, investigators must apply these procedures to their own reasoning: the theory predicts why their recursive inquiry will fail under defensive commitments and become possible under learning-oriented commitments. Recorded interventions then inform correction of both practice and explanatory claims. This anticipates the central mechanism of theoretically conditioned recursive self-testing, rather than merely advocating reflexivity. I cannot establish from memory that these publications satisfy a strict requirement for explicitly dated framework versions. That potentially distinguishes the documentation configuration, but does not establish novelty of the core relationship between substantive theory and its own recursive testability.
