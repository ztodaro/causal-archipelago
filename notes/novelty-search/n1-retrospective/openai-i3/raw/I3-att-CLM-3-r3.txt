PRIOR-ART: Helly’s theorem (published 1923); the Čech–Vietoris–Rips distinction, applied to communication networks in Vin de Silva and Robert Ghrist, “Coverage in sensor networks via persistent homology” (2007).
REASONING: The core mechanism is classical intersection geometry. A Čech/nerve simplex requires a common intersection of every represented region; a Vietoris–Rips/clique simplex requires only pairwise compatibility. Three disks can intersect pairwise without a triple intersection, so completing a communication graph into cliques can invent collective witnesses. Helly’s theorem formalizes when higher-order intersection tests guarantee a total intersection; pairwise tests generally do not. Recasting these regions as causal access regions supplies an interpretation rather than a new mechanism. A spacetime-specific result would require additional hypotheses: every finite collection of events in unrestricted Minkowski spacetime has a common future receiver, so an obstruction needs restrictions such as deadlines, horizons, or constrained receiver locations.
