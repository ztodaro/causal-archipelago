PRIOR-ART: Chris Argyris, Robert Putnam, and Diana McLain Smith, Action Science (1985), building on Argyris and Schön’s theory of organizational learning.
REASONING: Action science substantively explains how defensive reasoning obstructs valid inquiry and how alternative governing commitments enable it. Those predictions apply to action scientists’ own inquiry: their interventions can reproduce precisely the failures their theory diagnoses. Its operational methods expose researchers’ reasoning and interventions to recursive examination through recorded interactions, explicit interpretations, and revision following discrepancies. This anticipates the central relationship between substantive theory, conditions of successful self-application, and empirical correction. I would not claim these publications establish all five conditions in the proposed terminology, especially formal versioning. However, adding dated provenance and a version discipline does not establish a new core mechanism. The exact checklist and name could be original while the mechanism is anticipated.
