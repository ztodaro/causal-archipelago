PRIOR-ART: Euclidean minimax facility location and the smallest-enclosing-ball problem: Elzinga–Hearn’s published minimax-location algorithms (1972), and Welzl, “Smallest enclosing disks (balls and ellipsoids)” (1991). Unequal emission times give the additively weighted Euclidean 1-center problem.
REASONING: For target events (x_i,t_i), an unrestricted common receipt event minimizes T subject to ||x−x_i||≤c(T−t_i) for every i. This is the standard second-order-cone formulation of an additively weighted Euclidean 1-center problem. Simultaneous emissions reduce exactly to the smallest enclosing ball. The proposed higher-order tightness already appears in its classical support structure: an equilateral triangle requires receipt time s/(√3c), although every pair needs only s/(2c); a regular tetrahedron requires √6s/(4c). Thus triples and tetrahedra binding beyond pairwise constraints are established geometric phenomena. Maximizing included target weight at each deadline is a coverage/subset-selection layer over this feasibility problem. Collector-strategy constraints could contribute new results, but the stated common-witness mechanism and analytic small-case structure reconstruct existing optimization geometry.
