VERDICT: NOT-NOVEL
CONFIDENCE: 96
PRIOR-ART: Pittordis & Sutherland’s Gaia wide-binary gravity tests; Banik et al. (2024), “Strong constraints on the gravitational law from Gaia DR3 wide binaries”; established simulation-based null calibration and error-convolved forward modelling.
REASONING: The core mechanism—inject observational noise into simulated data, apply the actual estimator, and compare observations against the resulting reference distribution—is established statistical practice. Published wide-binary gravity analyses already use simulated Newtonian and modified-gravity populations with observational effects to distinguish apparent velocity excesses from physical departures. Recomputing a scalar reference value instead of a full distribution does not create a new mechanism. The approximately +0.10 shift could be a useful result for this particular estimator and sample, but it is not itself methodological novelty. Cryptographically timestamped preregistration, fixed statistics, and prespecified limits on inference improve credibility; their combination with noise calibration is a procedural contribution rather than a novel gravity-testing mechanism.
