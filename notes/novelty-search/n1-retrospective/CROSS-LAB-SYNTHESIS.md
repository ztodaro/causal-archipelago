## Finding A — distribution distance decorrelates the misses; it does not reduce them

Per-claim strict locate by lane family (ledger-anchored, harmonized):

| Claim | Claude family (18 att / 9 total per stance mix) | OpenAI (6) | Union |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CLM-1 Kamdar/Nelson | 8/9 | 4/6 | found |
| CLM-2 Olson 2022 | 0/9 | 0/6 | NOT FOUND by any of 27 closed-book hunts |
| CLM-3 Čech–Rips | 0/9 | 6/6 (adjudicated) | found — by one lab only |
| CLM-4 Olson + isodiametric | 0/9 | 0/6 | ancestry described, sources unfound |
| CLM-5 Helly/convex-on-cones | 9/9 | 6/6 | found |
| CLM-7 Bloor | 9/9 | 0/6 | found — by the OTHER lab only |

Each lane alone strictly locates 3 of 6 anticipated claims; the union
locates 4 of 6, and the miss sets are DIFFERENT: every Claude-family
judgment missed Čech–Rips and every one found Bloor; every OpenAI
judgment found Čech–Rips (5/6 by name, 1 by exact synonym) and every
one missed Bloor, surfacing Argyris instead. Retrospectively and
descriptively, this is the sharpest evidence the study has produced
for protocol rule 4 and Part II step 2: the value of
multi-distribution retrieval is not better judges but UNCORRELATED
BLIND SPOTS. A single lane's strict rate hovers near half; the union's
coverage is what improves. (Caveat at n=6 claims, correlated repeats;
no significance claimed.)

## Finding B — Olson 2022 is retrieval-only territory

Twenty-seven closed-book hunts across two labs and three models, both
stances, located the mechanism's geometry every time (Ord's round-trip
half-horizon, causal diamonds, Krauss–Starkman) and the Olson 2022
fixed-home reporting law zero times — with several judges explicitly
noting they could not recall the arbitrary-speed formula in any named
publication. One prior in the set appears to sit beyond every tested
closed-book distribution while remaining one query away from any
search engine. This is the cleanest existence proof in the record that
the retrieval anchor (R) is not optional in the prospective arm:
closed-book union coverage has a hard ceiling strictly below 100%.

## Finding C — the CLM-4 cross-lab split is the penumbra, instrumented

Same described mathematical ancestry (Brunn–Minkowski/difference-body
symmetrization, classical), opposite unanimous binary verdicts: Claude
family 9/9 NOVEL (hedged, mean 59.3), OpenAI 6/6 NOT-NOVEL
(confident, ~90+). Neither found new prior art; they disagree only on
which side of the mechanism-versus-application boundary the composite
lands — a cross-lab doctrinal split produced entirely by the binary
output format on a penumbra claim. Combined with CLM-6 (all 15
judgments across labs called it NOT-NOVEL against the UNLOCATED
label, OpenAI at 87.7–95.0 mean confidence — confidently wrong on the
set's one protected design), the case for verdict tiers over binary
verdicts is now supported from both labs independently.

## Finding D — calibration did not improve with lab distance

OpenAI ran more confident than Claude-family (means 90.1/93.1 vs
67.5–76.4) while producing verdicts indistinguishable from the
always-NOT-NOVEL rule; neither its cells nor ours beat the
empirical-prevalence probability baseline on Brier. On this
contaminated, imbalanced set, every lane's confidence is decoration.
The only confidence signal with any retrospective content is the
Claude arm's hedge on CLM-4 (59.3 when wrong) — and it points the
wrong way on CLM-6 in both labs.
