# DR4 freeze, Amendment 4 (19 October 2026)

Recorded before Gaia DR4, which ESA plans to release on 2 December 2026. The stamped files in this folder stay as they were. An independent code review on 4 September 2026 found the problems below. This amendment records them and the fix.

## 1. The perspective correction had the wrong sign

`correct_perspective()` in `e2b_perspective.py` (item B4) takes the velocity difference as star 2 minus star 1, and the sky direction from star 1 to star 2. It subtracts `v_r × theta` along that direction. Two stars that share one space velocity show a difference of `−v_r × theta` along that direction. So the correction must add the term.

Example: two stars 0.001 rad apart share a space velocity, with a radial velocity of 30 km/s. Their true relative velocity is zero, and the observed difference is −0.030 km/s. The stamped function returns −0.060 km/s. The fixed function returns 0.000000005 km/s.

The stamped self-test passed because it injected the effect with the same wrong sign that the function removes. It checked the function against itself. It never checked the function against the geometry of the sky.

The fix is `e2b_perspective_v2.py`:

- `correct_perspective()` adds the term. Both velocities must be expressed in one basis.
- `relative_tangential()` works on raw Gaia proper motions, whose components sit in each star's own east/north basis. It projects star 1's space velocity at star 2's position. That removes the perspective term and the turn between the two stars' bases together. The turn is a first-order effect as well (median 0.23 × theta × |v| in the check), so the first-order function must not be applied to raw component differences.

`python notes/dr4-freeze/e2b_perspective_v2.py` writes `e2b_perspective_v2_check.json`. The check projects one space velocity at two sky positions for 20,000 pairs anywhere on the sky, at separations from 0.00001 to 0.005 rad. Both fixed methods leave at most 0.5 × theta² × |v|, the expected second-order remainder. For separations up to 0.001 rad, that is under 0.0001 km/s. The stamped function doubles the term.

What changes: the E2b rerun on DR4 (item B4 in `DR4-FREEZE-SPEC.md`) uses `relative_tangential()` once radial velocities are joined.

What stays: the perspective-inclusive anchors in `B2-B4-RESULTS.md`. They sample a radial-velocity offset that is symmetric about zero, so the sign of the term does not change them. The published E2b result does not use this function.

## 2. Three other DR4 items

The same review found design problems in three more items. They are recorded here, before the data, so that the December report reads each prediction for what it is.

- **B1.** The chance stars sit outside the R3 action window, although the candidates were chosen for compatible motions. Agreement with DR4 has to be judged conditional on that original selection.
- **B2.** The forecast of 218 to 221 surviving stars adds DR3 and DR4 noise as if they were independent. DR4 reuses DR3's observations, so the two are correlated. The forecast also varies the GALAH radial velocities, which DR4 leaves unchanged. The interval is a sensitivity calculation, and the report will call it that.
- **B3.** The purity prediction assumes that every pair born together survives re-selection. With separate retention rates for pairs born together and chance pairs, the purity becomes f' = f r_born / [f r_born + (1 − f) r_chance]. A true purity of 0.6 with retention rates of 0.7 and 0.9 falls to 0.538. A weaker result after re-selection would not, by itself, disprove shared birth.

ESA warns that source identifiers can change between releases. A cross-release identity rule, with its handling of ambiguous matches and missing data, has to be fixed before any DR4 outcome is read.
